Two practical findings on the way:

- A `t` level's kernel set is ~16x an `n` set's (1.35M bodies against 85k;
  exact floor states x timers), ~13 GB resident; the default keeps every
  prebuilt set, so four `t` sets alone filled the cap. `CELESTE_KERNEL_SETS=1`
  (rebuild per level, ~75 s) is required for `t` ladders in this room. And the
  prebuild of all finer levels at once (16 walk threads each) ran out of memory
  before any frame: `CELESTE_KERNEL_SETS` caps that too.
- `rewrite witness --level K` takes K as `Bits(K)` with everything else exact;
  it cannot follow a `CELESTE_LADDER` level (n, t, h), so there is no cheap way
  yet to see WHICH over-approximation the f164 coarse wins use. That is the
  next tool to build: a witness/`spurious` that takes the ladder spec.

### Why the coarse levels win early: sliver buckets (a real mechanism)

`ancestry --chain-out` + `trajectory --spec` realize one abstract chain
concretely. The h116 level 7 (r6) chain to f105: its f52 row is a sibling of
the reference's (same f51 row), differing only in rem.x: from rem.x in
[-0.40625, -0.39064] (one 1/64 bucket) and spd.x = 1 - 1/65536, the move gives
[-0.40627, -0.39066] - one raw unit over the bucket boundary - so the kernel
emits a second row whose rem.x is the SLIVER [-0.40627, -0.40625), which the
boundary widens to the whole bucket [-0.421875, -0.40625). No concrete state is
in it. From there the abstract run holds a position up to a bucket lower than
any real one. A move straddles a boundary on most frames, so the abstract can
gain up to one bucket width per frame per axis: ~120 airborne frames at rem 6
is ~1.9 px - corners cleared, walls reached early. It also explains the
doubling (every straddle is two rows) and why a halving of the bucket only
halves the drift: a long room needs many rungs. (At rem 1 the chain was
already off at f53: (14,7) where every concretization of the row reaches at
most (14,6).)

### An unsound kernel op: interval +/- wrapped (branch `asm-interval-wrap`)

The second half's refutation of its own known solution: `follow --start-after`
through level 12 shows the reference's step at f16 (room frame 129) is a
kernel successor FILTERED by level 11's marks; through level 11 (`t`), the
real successor - fall_floor (40,120) going from shaking (1) to fallen (2) - is
not a successor at all. Under `t` the floor's `delay` is the whole 16.16
range and the cart does `delay = delay - 1` before `delay <= 0`: the
assembled interval subtraction wrapped the low end (plain vpsubd; the Rust
primitives panic instead, and the ASM tests bound their columns so nothing
ever overflowed) to [32767.99, 32766.99], and `<= 0` came out "no". Shaking
floors never fell, fallen ones never returned - every `t` level of this room
was unsound.

Fixed on `asm-interval-wrap` (72fdea7: an overflowing endpoint gives the
whole range, in the kernel and the primitives; a test pins it) but NOT merged:
without the wrap, the countdown comparison is undecided where the `n` kernels
need it decided, and room (1,1) `r0sxhn` declines lanes at f35. The `n` levels
widen floor state and collideable at every boundary, which re-adds what the
wrap removed, so the committed `n`-ladder results are believed sound - not
proven. Before merging: make the countdowns under `n`/`t` FORK on `delay <= 0`
(or keep them out of interval arithmetic), then re-run the `n` gates.
